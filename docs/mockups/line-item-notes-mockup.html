Two discount states, as you asked. Confirmed strikes the list price through and moves the total. To be confirmed shows the rate being chased and what it would come to, but moves no figure, so the client sees it without the budget promising it. A consultant who doubts a discount will happen at all picks None. Nothing produces a range any more, which makes the project total a great deal firmer than the first pass.

Two

Framing becomes a sub line item

Framing is already a sub line item, it just has hard-coded plumbing: one per artwork, no VAT switch, no note. Making it one kind among several costs nothing in the display and gives duty, shipping and crating somewhere to live.

Why this beats a project-level line. A cost attached to a work travels with it. If the client picks a different option, duty on the Dublin sprinters leaves with the sprinters. A project-level Import duty line would stay behind and quietly be wrong. Keep a kind on each sub item so framing can still have its own row in the summary.

What the sub-item change costs

Framing is special-cased in eight files. The artwork carries a framed or requires-framing status and a cost, the studio sidebar has a two-button toggle for it, and the summary has its own Framing row. Turning it into a sub item touches all of that.

It is worth doing in two steps. Add sub items first and copy the existing framing values into one, while the old columns stay put and keep working. Then, once the app reads only the sub item, a second migration drops the old columns. Additive first, destructive last, which is the order the deploy guide already asks for.
